## Deluin — The Wall on the Inside

**In one breath:** Deluin builds walls, so when his parents opened Redshire's gate to the Arctics, he walked through it with them and became a wall on the inside, and he has never told them which way it faces.

Every spring the path to the great icy waterfall in Redshire's red mountains is half buried by rockfall, and every spring a trail crew rebuilds the low stone wall that keeps walkers off the ice. At eleven, Deluin was sent to haul stones for them as punishment for telling a visitor that praising a country for its scenery is an insult, since nobody there made the mountains. After a week the crew chief asked him who he thought had made the path he was standing on. He kept the answer, and the lesson of the wall: the storm wins every winter, and the wall is still there every summer. He still plays Minpentai that way. Walls first, then let the storm come and turn it back.

His parents believed you keep the peace by agreeing with whoever is strongest, and when the Arctics came, they agreed. Deluin could have run. He got on the plane to the Arctic core with them instead, wearing their good name as a disguise, and in Glasscrown he built walls out of words, every slogan right and every skill on show, until the Empire gave him its southern flank. Then he left a gap in it exactly wide enough for his friend from Dzego.

So the boy who never lets a glider through his walls won the most important game of his life by opening one, and he keeps his parents safe by lying to them. In Dzego they turned his name into "De lu hin", a fork with the appearance of, and he called it lovely. A fork is one road that seems to go two ways. When Zei finally beat him, on a battlefield Deluin had laid out himself, he quoted the old line: when I am defeated, I feel like I won.
